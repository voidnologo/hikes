The shape of the trip

Two-thirds gentle riverside (the BMT shares the John Muir Trail along the Hiwassee for ~10.5 mi), one-third climb up Unicoi Mountain at the end. Almost all the climbing is in the final 9 miles after Coker Creek. The mid-August play: do the cool, shaded, water-rich river miles first, save the climb for a dawn start on the last day, and finish with a view.

Water

Water is plentiful for the first ~10 miles. Coker Creek (mile ~10) is the last reliable water and the last established campsite. After it, there's one spring just south of the TN-68 crossing, then nothing until Unicoi Gap. Both camps are on the river half; you climb out carrying water.

Day by day

Day 1 — Childers Creek to Towee Creek area

~7 mi · easy

Afternoon drop-off works — a short, flat riverside day. Highlights: The Narrows (deep canyon, old riverbed rock formations) and the swinging bridge at the Apalachia Powerhouse. Camp at a riverside primitive site; swim and filter from the river.

Day 2 — Towee Creek area to Coker Creek (FR 22B)

~5–6 mi · easy

Short, relaxed day past Big Bend. Camp at the Coker Creek primitive site — boulders, swimming, the confluence. Tank up all your water here (3–4 L each) for tomorrow's dry climb. Optional: Coker Creek Falls side trip (~5.4 mi RT) to a 40-ft cascade.

Day 3 — Coker Creek to Unicoi Gap (via TN-68)

~9 mi · the climb · pickup

Start at dawn. Nearly all the elevation gain, dry, hottest exposure — an early start beats the heat. Top off water at the spring ~0.2 mi south of the TN-68 crossing (last water before the top). Optional payoff: the Buck Bald side trip (360° panorama) before dropping to the gap. Finish at Unicoi Gap for pickup.

Heat-safety variant

If a 9-mi dry climb on the last day sounds like too much, push past Coker Creek on Day 2 and dry-camp near the TN-68 spring (carry water up), making Day 3 a short ~4.7 mi to Unicoi Gap. Tradeoff: you haul camp water and lose a riverside night.

River exit variant

Skip Unicoi Mountain entirely. Where the BMT splits off to climb (the Unicoi Mountain Trail junction, ~10.5 mi in), stay on the John Muir Trail and follow it to its eastern end at Brushy Creek near Farner — 20.7 mi total, riverside to mile 17.7, then about three miles over the ridge past Millers Cove and down a set of switchbacks. It trades the 9-mile dry climb for water nearly the whole way, which is the mid-August argument for it. Costs: no Buck Bald and no finish at the gap. The trail ends near 35.18771, −84.30387, about 2.7 mi south of Unicoi Gap. The drive in is TN-68 → Old State Highway 68 → Prince Ferry Road, which runs northeast along the north bank to the Brushy Creek crossing and continues into North Carolina as State Road 1322. Two things still to settle: exactly where the trail meets the road at that end, and trail condition — the eastern half sees far less traffic than the Reliance end, so expect overgrowth and blowdowns.

Trailheads

Point-to-point, so you need a drop-off and a pickup. Send your drivers the coordinates rather than the place names — both ends are easy to mis-map.

Verify before you go

Cherokee NF fire restrictions (call the Ocoee/Hiwassee Ranger District: 423-476-9700), the TVA Apalachia weekend release schedule (riverside camping safety), the Unicoi Gap gravel road status, and recent FarOut water comments for the Coker Creek → Unicoi Gap stretch.
